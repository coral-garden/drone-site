# Landing-page content strategy

## Scope

Production homepage: `index.html`.

The old selected-direction route at `directions/fieldcraft.html` redirects to the production homepage.

Alternate Precision landing page: `directions/precision.html`. It shares the confirmed AirWorks offer and audience strategy while preserving the more technical Precision visual direction.

Fieldcraft is now an information-first field-operations brief with a grayscale interface and selected operational photography retained in color. It uses a self-hosted Manrope sans-serif type system, readable utility text, simple rules, and factual content blocks. Do not add generic regional or scenic stock photography. Photography should show a relevant drone operation, aircraft, operator, payload, output, or inspected asset, with source and license credits. Avoid decorative flight diagrams, oversized editorial slogans, dashboard styling, and accent colors until real project evidence provides a reason to introduce them.

This strategy uses the current repository plus the owner's confirmed launch brief:

- the company name is AirWorks Drone Services;
- it is based in Samal and serves Mindanao;
- LGUs are the primary audience, with farms and other project teams as secondary audiences;
- the owner confirms that AirWorks directly operates its drones, including agricultural operations for spraying/application, seeding and granular spreading, crop monitoring, multispectral/NDVI imaging, and farm mapping;
- the core public service areas are agricultural drone operations, mapping and survey support, progress monitoring, infrastructure and asset inspection, and aerial photo/video, with other drone work scoped per project;
- for professional survey work, AirWorks operates the drone and captures the aerial data alongside a licensed geodetic engineer;
- email and phone are the intended contact routes, with launch details still to be supplied;
- credentials, portfolio proof, equipment, turnaround times, and pricing are not yet confirmed;
- the selected brand direction is roughly 80% grounded Fieldcraft and 20% technical Precision.

## Page purpose

Turn a visitor with a site, structure, property, or visual brief into a qualified project enquiry.

**Primary action:** send site details by email or phone once the placeholder contact details are replaced.

**Secondary action:** understand which service and output fits the job.

## Primary audience

LGUs in Mindanao that need agricultural drone operations, aerial data, or imagery for mapping, planning, monitoring, inspection, or communication.

Secondary audiences include farms and agricultural operators, survey and engineering teams, planners, site managers, contractors, developers, utility and facility operators, property teams, tourism teams, and communications teams. Do not let cinematic work dominate the main argument unless it becomes the commercial priority.

## Positioning

A Samal-based drone-services company providing agricultural operations, mapping and survey support, monitoring, inspection, aerial media, and project-scoped drone work for LGUs, farms, and project teams across Mindanao.

The primary pitch is broad, directly operated drone capability backed by field judgment and clear outputs. Keep the licensed-geodetic-engineer relationship in the mapping and survey-support details rather than the hero.

## Core page argument

1. **The operation is defined before takeoff.** Start with what the client needs to apply, map, monitor, inspect, document, or communicate.
2. **The place shapes the plan.** Terrain, weather, access, timing, and the surrounding community are part of fieldwork in Mindanao.
3. **Each service should produce a defined handoff.** Coverage, viewpoint, repetition, detail, and file organisation follow the intended use.
4. **Local knowledge should feel practical, not ornamental.** Mindanao belongs in site language, imagery, planning details, and service scope—not in generic “proudly local” claims.
5. **The next step is a clear brief.** Ask for the location and need before asking the visitor to understand drone terminology.

## Messaging pillars

### 1. Useful after the flight

Lead with decisions and deliverables. Explain what mapping capture, progress imagery, inspection visuals, or aerial media help the client see or do next.

### 2. Planned around the ground

Use concrete field factors: terrain, access, weather window, light, timing, surroundings, and repeat viewpoints. Avoid vague claims such as “anywhere, anytime.”

### 3. Clear scope and handoff

The page should signal that coverage and files are agreed per project. This builds confidence without inventing turnaround times, file formats, or technical tolerances.

### 4. Mindanao-based, Philippine in practice

Root the language in how projects are actually located and coordinated here: barangay, city or municipality, province, a map pin or coordinates, a site contact, and the target date. Use these details in the enquiry brief rather than forcing local slang into headlines.

## Philippine voice and localisation rules

- Use direct, professional English with a warm, straightforward Philippine cadence.
- Do not use forced Tagalog as shorthand for being Filipino. Mindanao is linguistically diverse; add Cebuano, Filipino, or another local language only when the intended audience and a fluent reviewer are known.
- Prefer familiar project language: site, property, location, construction progress, exterior areas, access, handoff, target date.
- Avoid imported startup language such as “revolutionise,” “unlock,” “seamless,” “cutting-edge,” and “elevate your vision.”
- Avoid tourism clichés that flatten Mindanao into scenery.
- Never imply the Unsplash images are company portfolio work. Keep location and photographer captions factual; replace them with approved project photography when available.
- “Serving Mindanao” and “across Mindanao” are supported by the owner's confirmed service area; do not expand this to “nationwide” or invent city-specific coverage.
- Do not claim CAAP compliance, permits, insurance, certifications, survey-grade accuracy, RTK/PPK capability, or specific outputs until supporting evidence is supplied.
- AirWorks may state that survey work is undertaken with a licensed geodetic engineer and that AirWorks handles drone operations and aerial data capture. Do not present AirWorks alone as a surveying firm or promise boundaries, measurements, engineering accuracy, or certified survey deliverables without a documented project workflow.

## Voice

- **Grounded:** sounds like someone who has prepared for field conditions.
- **Exact:** uses specific nouns and verbs instead of technical theatre.
- **Calm:** no hype, urgency, or inflated adjectives.
- **Locally aware:** recognises the realities of Philippine sites without stereotyping.

Suggested balance: 70% practical, 20% technical, 10% regional character.

## Recommended page flow

### 1. Hero and core positioning

State the broad offer, Samal base, Mindanao service area, and primary client groups in the heading and supporting copy. Place the title block over one credited color aerial image relevant to Mindanao operations. Keep contact details in the project-enquiry section rather than adding a separate hero facts table.

Primary CTA once a contact route exists: **Send site details**.
Secondary CTA: **View services**.

### 2. Detailed services

Give each core service a plain description, intended client group, and concrete scope list:

- **Agricultural drone operations:** crop spraying/application, seeding and granular spreading, crop monitoring, multispectral/NDVI imaging, and farm mapping.
- **Mapping and survey support:** flight and coverage planning, aerial data capture, project-specific files, and coordination with a licensed geodetic engineer when the work is a professional survey.
- **Progress monitoring:** repeat viewpoints, site context, dated and organised files, and reporting imagery.
- **Infrastructure and asset inspection:** overview and detail imagery of roads, bridges, buildings, roofs, towers, utilities, and other difficult-to-view assets for review by project and technical teams. Do not present the imagery itself as an engineering assessment.
- **Aerial photo and video:** property, event, project, and location coverage.
- **Other drone operations:** invite a project brief rather than claiming every possible capability; scope the required aircraft, payload or sensor, site conditions, operating requirements, and handoff.

### 3. Operational photography when available

Use one image for each core service: an agricultural operation, mapping or survey flight, construction-progress aerial, infrastructure aerial, and aerial-media example. Keep them in color against the grayscale interface. Put full source, creator, license, and modification details on `credits.html`, linked clearly from the footer, rather than repeating attribution inside every service row. Do not imply third-party reference images are AirWorks portfolio work. Do not use generic landscape or tourism imagery.

### 4. Work by client type

Show how the offer applies to LGUs; farms and agricultural teams; survey, engineering, and planning teams; construction and development teams; utilities and facility operators; and property, tourism, and communications teams. Describe possible project needs without implying past clients or results.

### 5. Project workflow and survey roles

Explain brief → site review → operation plan → handoff. Keep the survey-role clarification here and within mapping details, not in the primary pitch.

### 6. Proof when available

The current page has no verified proof. Before launch, prioritise:

1. approved project images labelled with barangay/city or municipality/province;
2. one short project note per priority service: need, operation plan, and delivered material;
3. verified pilot, business, regulatory, insurance, equipment, and agricultural-system information where commercially relevant;
4. actual deliverable examples;
5. client quotations only with permission.

Do not use stock photography as a substitute for proof.

### 7. Project enquiry

Ask for:

- barangay and city/municipality/province, or a map pin;
- LGU office, farm, team, or organisation;
- service required or intended result;
- crop, site, structure, or asset details;
- estimated area or project scale;
- target date and access information.

Recommended CTA once connected: **Send site details**.

## Objections the eventual copy should answer

- Is this service suitable for my site or structure?
- What will I receive?
- Can the same view be repeated for progress tracking?
- What information is needed for a quote or flight plan?
- Where does the company operate?
- How do weather, access, and site conditions affect timing?
- Is mapping capture the same as a legal survey? It should not be presented that way.

Only answer these with confirmed operating details.

- farm mapping and crop monitoring;
- aerial mapping capture in Mindanao;

Create city or provincial pages only for locations genuinely served and only when each page has useful local content. Do not generate thin pages for Davao, Cagayan de Oro, General Santos, or other cities merely to rank.

Suggested description: **Samal-based agricultural drone operations, mapping, monitoring, inspection, and aerial media for LGUs, farms, and project teams across Mindanao.**
