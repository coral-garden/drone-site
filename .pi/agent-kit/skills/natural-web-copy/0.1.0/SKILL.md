# Natural web copy

Make the page sound like a specific business speaking to a specific customer.
Preserve facts, offer terms, links, names, prices, quotations, and qualifiers.

## Write

- Lead with the customer's job or outcome in language they use.
- Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs. Turn features into evidenced customer
  consequences without inventing proof.
- Give each section one purpose and one new piece of information.
- Make calls to action say what the visitor gets or does next.
- Match established brand voice and nearby page rhythm. Use contractions and
  personality only when they belong to that voice.

## Remove slop

- Delete generic praise, abstract significance, unsupported superlatives, and
  sentences that could describe any company.
- Replace vague authority and fake certainty with a named source, a qualified
  claim, or no claim.
- Rewrite stock AI shapes: “not just X, but Y,” forced groups of three,
  repetitive summary conclusions, superficial `-ing` clauses, and synonym
  cycling.
- Prefer `is`, `has`, and direct subjects over “serves as,” “stands as,”
  “boasts,” “showcases,” “elevates,” or “unlocks.”
- Vary sentence length without fragments that hide the actor. Avoid em dashes
  unless the established brand voice clearly uses them.
- Keep useful repetition when it preserves the customer's own term. Do not
  rename the same concept for variety.

Read the result aloud. Every sentence must add a fact, argument, instruction,
or deliberate voice beat. Cut the rest.

## Boundaries

Never invent customers, testimonials, metrics, features, guarantees, urgency,
scarcity, awards, or differentiation. Natural language cannot repair missing
evidence. Return unsupported claims and missing product decisions to the
parent. Do not publish or deploy.
